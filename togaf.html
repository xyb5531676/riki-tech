What is TOGAF?

The Global Standard for
Enterprise Architecture

TOGAF® (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is used to help organisations design an IT infrastructure that is adapted to meet their specific requirements. This is where trained TOGAF® professionals come in — they communicate with various department heads and help design and implement an IT strategy quickly and effectively.

TOGAF provides a comprehensive framework and set of tools for developing enterprise architecture. It uses the Architecture Development Method (ADM) as its core process — a step-by-step approach to developing, implementing, and maintaining an enterprise architecture.

TOGAF® (The Open Group Architecture Framework) certified Enterprise Architects at Riki-Tech provide EA (Enterprise Architect) consulting services — from initial maturity assessment through to full architecture repository delivery.

ADM Phases

Architecture Development Method

The TOGAF ADM is the core of the TOGAF framework — a step-by-step process for developing enterprise architecture. Riki-Tech follows this rigorously in every engagement.

P

Preliminary Phase

Preparation and initiation — customisation of the TOGAF framework and definition of Architecture Principles for the organisation.

A

Phase A: Architecture Vision

Defines scope of the development initiative, identifies stakeholders, creates the Architecture Vision, and obtains approval to proceed.

B

Phase B: Business Architecture

Develops a Business Architecture to support the agreed Architecture Vision — business processes, functions, roles and governance.

C

Phase C: Information Systems Architectures

Develops Information Systems Architectures — data and application architectures — to support the Architecture Vision.

D

Phase D: Technology Architecture

Develops the Technology Architecture — IT infrastructure, middleware, networks, communications and processing standards.

E

Phase E: Opportunities & Solutions

Conducts initial implementation planning and identifies delivery vehicles — work packages, projects, and transition architectures.

F

Phase F: Migration Planning

Addresses how to move from Baseline to Target Architectures by finalising a detailed Implementation and Migration Plan.

G

Phase G: Implementation Governance

Provides architectural oversight of the implementation — ensuring conformance with the target architecture throughout delivery.

H

Phase H: Architecture Change Management

Establishes procedures for managing change to the new architecture — ensuring it remains fit for purpose over time.

RM

Requirements Management

Examines the process of managing architecture requirements throughout the ADM — the central hub connecting all phases.

Architecture Domains

Four Layers of Enterprise Architecture

There are four architecture domains commonly accepted as subsets of an overall Enterprise Architecture. The TOGAF standard is designed to support all four, and our Riki-Tech EA Consulting Service covers all these layers.

🏢

Business Architecture

Defines the business strategy, governance, organisation, and key business processes — the foundation of the entire EA.

🗄️

Data Architecture

Describes the structure of an organisation's logical and physical data assets and data management resources.

⚙️

Application Architecture

Provides a blueprint for individual applications to be deployed, their interactions, and relationships to core business processes.

🖥️

Technology Architecture

Describes logical software and hardware capabilities required to support deployment of business, data, and application services.
